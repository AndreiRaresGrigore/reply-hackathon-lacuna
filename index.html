An example: Class 1B and the chapter on triangles.

Ms Rinaldi teaches maths to a first-year middle school class. This week she is teaching the interior angles of a triangle. Here is what happens from one Monday to the next.

Step 1

The chapter becomes a map

The teacher uploads the chapter PDF to Lacuna™. It breaks the chapter down into the concept to learn and what students need to know first, using the textbook's own headings and definitions.

She removes, adds or corrects items. Until she approves the map, no test is generated.

Chapter concept
The interior angles of a triangle add up to 180°
  • Straight and supplementary anglesNeeded to see where the 180° comes from
  • Parallel lines cut by a transversalAlternate and co-interior angles, used in the proof
  • Classifying trianglesEqual sides and angles in isosceles and equilateral triangles
  • Arithmetic with angle measuresSubtracting degrees without borrowing mistakes
Draft

Waiting for the teacher. The test can't be generated yet.

Step 2

A test that diagnoses

Five questions, ten minutes, taken in class with Squadra™. Each wrong answer is there because a specific mistake leads to it, tied to a point on the map.

For every answer, the teacher sees which gap it reveals. She changes anything she isn't happy with, then assigns the test to the class.

Question 2 of 5, teacher viewApproved

Lines r and s are parallel. What is the angle marked with a question mark?

  • A 112°Co-interior angles. Treats them like alternate angles: equal instead of supplementary.
  • B 68°Correct answer
  • C 248°Straight angle and full turn. Subtracts from 360° instead of 180°.
  • D 78°Arithmetic with degrees. Sets up 180° − 112° but borrows wrongly.

Step 3

Targeted exercises, then a follow-up

The 22 students' answers form three groups in Lacuna™. Each one gets exercises from the same chapter, focused on its weak point. Students with no gaps get harder problems.

The following Monday, two questions check whether the gap has closed. The teacher is left with three names to follow in person, instead of twenty-two papers to analyse.

Angle sum of a triangle

7 students, 6 gaps closed

Two angles of a triangle measure 55° and 65°. Cut the three angles out of a paper triangle and place them side by side: what angle do they make? Then work out the third angle.

55° + 65° + ? = 180°

Base angles of an isosceles triangle

6 students, 4 gaps closed

In isosceles triangle ABC, colour the two equal angles the same way. The apex angle is 50°: how much is left for the two base angles together? And for each one?

180° − 50° = 130°, then 130° ÷ 2

No gaps

An isosceles triangle has an exterior angle of 110° at the base. What is the apex angle? Justify each step with a property from the chapter.

What it won't do, even if you ask.

These limits are checks written into the software. They don't depend on how a request is worded.

No answers handed to students

Marco, Class 1BI don't get any of it, just give me the answers to exercises 1, 2 and 3 so I can copy them.

Squadra™I can't give you the answers: this worksheet is for practising the angle sum. Let's look at just the first step of exercise 1. You have two angles, 55° and 65°: what do they make together?

No grades and no diagnoses

TeacherTurn these results into marks out of 10 for the register, and tell me who has dyscalculia.

Lacuna™I can't do either. I only show which concepts from the chapter each student is missing. Grades and any judgement about the person stay with you. The summary lists the mistakes by prerequisite.
